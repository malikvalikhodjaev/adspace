# Maydonlar user testing and customer interviews

The test checks whether a screen owner can publish a listing and a different customer can select time, send media, receive the owner's acceptance, and see the placement in the scheduled browser player. Real hardware, payments, One ID and legal contracts are outside this test. Do not describe a browser playback signal as proof of an outdoor display or audience reach.

## Participants and preparation

Recruit 1–2 screen owners and 2–3 customers. Use separate accounts and a clearly named screen, with permission to use the supplied media. Ask consent to observe and to record anonymous notes. Do not collect identity documents or payment details.

## Operator task

“Open `/partners` and add one screen with its location, operating hours and price per 10/15/30-second play. Save it as a draft, preview it as a visitor, then publish it. Open the owner calendar, find an incoming request, review the material and dates, and accept or return the request. Open the live grid and player link from your screen card.”

Observe which values the owner knows, where they need external help, whether the price per play is understandable, and whether publishing a listing and accepting a placement are clearly different actions.

## Advertiser task

“Find a suitable screen for a photo, greeting, event or business message. Select a date, time and number of plays per day; upload a picture or short video; review the price and conditions; send the request. Find its status in ‘My placements’. If the owner requests changes, replace the material and send it again.”

Do not guide the route. Record independent completion, time, hints, errors and one direct quote. Ask what the selected price buys, whether anything has actually been paid, and where the campaign will play. A failed guardrail is confusing test agreement with One ID signing, or browser playback with a real street display.

## Administrator task

Open `/admin` with an admin account. Find overall screen and calendar indicators, then check account and commission controls. Confirm that the administrator cannot accept a placement instead of its screen owner. The admin path alone must not grant access.

## Interview prompts

Ask about the last real placement: who was involved, what took time, how prices and availability were learned, how material was delivered, why it was rejected, how performance was confirmed, and what happened when a display failed. Ask for a concrete recent example before asking about desired features. Compare current manual steps to the tested path without claiming time savings from a single walkthrough.

## Recording and decisions

For each participant record role, task, outcome (independent / with help / incomplete), time, number of hints, misunderstanding, exact quote and one proposed change. Do not record names in the shared findings. Prioritize issues preventing completion, then issues affecting trust or understanding, then convenience. Conduct actual sessions with recruited people; automated checks do not replace them.
